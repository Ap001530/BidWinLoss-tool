# Discussion

This chapter interprets the Chapter 5 results together, sets out the thesis's limitations and threats to validity in full, and discusses what the work implies for the Engineering BU in practical terms, distinct from the strictly evaluative reporting of the previous chapter.

## Interpreting the Results Together

Three results from Chapter 5, read together rather than individually, form the thesis's central finding. First, the gradient-boosted model's cross-validated AUC (0.705) sits close to the dataset's own logistic-regression ceiling (approximately 0.72), which means the choice of a non-linear tree ensemble over a simple linear baseline bought comparatively little on this particular dataset, despite the data generator deliberately encoding non-linear interaction effects that a linear model cannot represent directly (Chapter 3). Second, the scorer's separate logistic surrogate achieved a cross-validated AUC (0.743) that is, if anything, marginally *higher* than the primary gradient-boosted model's. Third, the SHAP-versus-hypothesis Spearman correlation (0.242) is weak despite the two rankings sharing a common origin by construction.

The most defensible reading of these three results together is that the dataset, 500 bids, thirteen features, and a true win rate near 30%, is simply too small, relative to the number and subtlety of the effects encoded into it, for a more flexible model to reliably extract additional signal beyond what a well-regularised linear model already captures, and for a rank-correlation measure computed over only thirteen features to settle into a stable, strongly positive value even when the underlying model is behaving sensibly. This is not a failure of the pipeline; it is consistent with the general finding in the B2B win-prediction literature (Section 2.1) that datasets in this domain are characteristically small and noisy, and it is a genuine, informative result about what this thesis's synthetic data, deliberately built to be realistically small and noisy rather than large and clean, can and cannot show. A real Virtuos bid history, almost certainly larger over time and subject to a different, organisation-specific noise structure, may behave differently on both counts, which is precisely why Chapter 7 identifies moving to real data as the priority next step rather than further tuning this synthetic-data pipeline.

## Limitations

**The central limitation, repeated here because it governs how every other result in this thesis should be read, is that the dataset is synthetic.** Every number reported in Chapter 5, the AUC values, the SHAP ranking, the hypothesis comparison, is a statement about whether the pipeline correctly recovers a signal the author encoded, not a statement about what actually wins real Virtuos bids. This thesis has been explicit about this distinction throughout (Section 1.6, Chapter 3, and the artefact's own disclaimer banner), and it is restated here as a limitation rather than only as a scoping note, because it bounds every other finding: a reader who takes, for example, "requirement coverage ranks low in SHAP importance" as a real finding about Virtuos's actual bid dynamics would be misreading this thesis's claim.

**Dataset size.** Five hundred bids is small by machine-learning standards generally, and the 5-fold cross-validation's standard deviation of ± 0.052 around the mean AUC (Table 5.1) is itself evidence of that: a model's measured performance varies meaningfully depending on which 100 bids happen to fall in a given validation fold. A real Virtuos bid history, accumulated over a longer period, would very likely be larger, which both the literature (Section 2.1) and this thesis's own cross-validation variance suggest would materially improve the stability, and plausibly the ceiling, of any model built on it.

**The two scoping deviations from the original proposal.** Section 3.4 gave the full reasoning for building around a single gradient-boosted model rather than a Random Forest-versus-XGBoost comparison, and for a web dashboard rather than Power BI. Both are reiterated here as limitations rather than neutral choices: RQ2's answer in this thesis characterises one specific algorithm family's performance ceiling on this data, not a comparison across families, and the artefact does not demonstrate Power BI's enterprise deployment features (row-level security, scheduled refresh against a live source, integration into an existing BU reporting workspace). Both are treated in Chapter 7 as concrete, well-defined future work rather than as abandoned scope, precisely because the pipeline and the structured results file (Chapter 4) were built in a form that makes both extensions straightforward to add later without redoing the underlying work.

**The scorer's surrogate-model trade-off.** Section 5.5 found the linear surrogate's cross-validated AUC close to, even marginally above, the primary model's, but cautioned against generalising that result, since the surrogate cannot represent the interaction effects the data generator deliberately encodes (Chapter 3), only their average, additive approximation. Whether this trade-off remains small on a larger, real dataset, where interaction effects might be more strongly and more precisely estimable, is not established by this thesis.

**No formal user study.** RQ3 was evaluated structurally, against four concrete requirements derived from the research question itself (Section 5.6), rather than through a study with real BU stakeholders using the artefact to make real or simulated bid decisions. This was a necessary consequence of the absence of real bid data and real-decision context available to this thesis (Section 1.6), but it means the artefact's usability claims rest on the author's own structured review rather than on independent evidence from its intended users.

**Unresolved threshold and loss-reason granularity.** Section 5.1 identified the gap between precision and recall at the default 0.5 threshold as a direct consequence of class imbalance, and noted that a BU stakeholder could choose a different threshold depending on their risk tolerance; this thesis does not recommend a specific threshold or build threshold selection into the artefact. Relatedly, Zahid et al.'s [-@zahid2021predicting] finding that splitting losses into distinct reasons (no bid, client did not pursue, lost to competition) adds decision-relevant nuance beyond a flat Won/Lost label was noted in Chapter 2 but not implemented here; the synthetic data generator and the model both treat the outcome as strictly binary.

**The relationship-history non-monotonicity was designed but not directly confirmed.** As reported in Section 5.4, the pipeline's current evaluation output does not include a per-level breakdown of the relationship-history feature's effect, so whether the model specifically distinguished "prior client, poor delivery" from "new client" (the deliberately counter-intuitive pattern described in Chapter 3) rather than treating the feature as a simple monotonic scale is not established either way by the current evaluation.

## Threats to Validity

**Internal validity.** The strongest threat to internal validity in this thesis is the circularity risk inherent in validating a pipeline against data the same pipeline's author generated: because the data generator's weights, the model's training data, and the hypothesis comparison's reference ranking all trace back to the same source document (the BU's feature hypotheses), there is a structural risk of the evaluation confirming what it was built to confirm. This thesis addresses this risk in two ways rather than denying it exists: the data generator deliberately introduces noise, interaction effects, and a non-monotonic pattern specifically so the recovered signal is not a trivial restatement of the input (Chapter 3), and the SHAP-versus-hypothesis comparison is reported with its actual, weak correlation value (0.242) and a candid discussion of why, rather than a stronger correlation being engineered or implied. The threat is mitigated, not eliminated; it is eliminated only once the pipeline is run on real, independently recorded bid data, which this thesis does not have.

**External validity.** The results in Chapter 5 generalise, at most, to the claim that this pipeline's method, synthetic data design, model training, SHAP explanation, and artefact construction, functions correctly under the conditions tested. They do not generalise to a claim about real Virtuos bid dynamics, to other BUs within Virtuos, to other organisations in the co-development industry, or to other bidding contexts generally, each of which may have a different, unmodelled feature set, a different noise structure, and a different true win rate.

**Construct validity.** The thirteen features used (Chapter 3) are a reasonable operationalisation of the BU's documented hypotheses, but they are not a complete account of what might drive a real bid's outcome; features such as the specific individuals involved, the detailed technical content of a proposal, or macroeconomic conditions at the time of bidding are not represented, and a real-data implementation may find some of these matter more than any feature in the current set.

**Reliability.** The data generation, train/test split, and cross-validation fold assignment all use a fixed random seed, so the specific numbers reported in Chapter 5 are exactly reproducible by re-running the pipeline (Appendix C gives the exact commands), which supports this thesis's own internal reliability. It does not, however, establish that the *qualitative* findings (which features rank highly, how large the AUC gap between models is) would be robust to a different random seed; a single run with a different seed could plausibly shift the cross-validation fold AUCs enough to change, for example, whether the gradient-boosted model's ceiling reads as "close to" or "clearly above" the logistic baseline. This was not tested across multiple seeds and is noted as a reliability limitation.

## Implications for the Engineering BU

Read with the limitations above firmly in view, this thesis's practical implication for the Engineering BU is not "these are the features that win bids," which the synthetic-data caveat rules out as a claim this thesis can support, but a narrower, still useful one: a decision-support pipeline of this shape, data-to-model-to-SHAP-to-artefact, is buildable, runs end to end on data resembling a real bid log in structure and scale, and produces output (a global importance ranking, per-bid explanations, a live scorer) in a form the BU's own stakeholders can read without statistical training. The concrete next step this implies for the BU is not to act on any specific feature ranking in Chapter 5, but to begin systematically recording resolved bid outcomes against this same thirteen-feature structure, so that this pipeline can be re-run on real data as soon as a sufficient history exists, an action item made concrete in Chapter 7's recommendations.
